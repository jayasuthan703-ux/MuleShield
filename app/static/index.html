        Myth vs reality

        What recruiters count on
        MythReality
        “I'm only renting my account for a few days — no real risk.” Your account moves money that was stolen from someone else. Accounts get frozen and you can be named in a complaint — renting does not create an alibi.
        “The money landed in my account, so it must be legal.” The money arriving is the crime's proceeds. Passing it on is exactly the step that turns a student account into a laundering trail.
        “10% commission for doing nothing — that's just easy money.” The commission is payment for taking someone else's legal risk. Nobody pays that much for a favour that is harmless.
        “I'm a student, the law won't come after me.” Being young or a student is no shield. The trail runs through your account and KYC details, not your intentions.
        “If I delete the chat, there's no evidence.” Banks keep the transaction records for years. Deleting messages only removes your side of the story.
        “Only greedy or careless people fall for this.” Recruiters target students with exam stress, fees to pay or a genuine need for part-time work — with scripts written by professionals.
        “A scam always asks me to pay money first, so I'll recognise it.” Mule recruitment pays you. That is the trap: you are never asked to send money, only to let money pass through.
